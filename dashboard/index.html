Different boundaries.

A straight line struggles to separate a ring from its center. A curved boundary can follow the shape of the data.

Plots show a sample of 100 points; scores use all 150 test points. One fixed synthetic split, not a general benchmark.

The third axis is the model’s answer.

These are two-dimensional datasets. The surface height shows the signed SVM decision score at each location. Its zero crossing produces the classification boundary on the floor.

01

Dots are observations

Filled dots trained the model. Open rings were held out for testing. Their positions never change between models.

02

The surface is computed

Every rise and dip comes from the fitted decision function. The displayed height is clipped at ±2.2 so the scale remains readable.

03

One split, one observation

The accuracy figures describe this fixed split. They are not a general performance estimate or a claim that one kernel always wins.

Built to be checked.

Each dataset has 500 synthetic observations. A stratified 70/30 split with seed 42 is reused across models. StandardScaler is fitted only on training data; SVC uses C = 1 and polynomial degree 3. Laplacian uses Manhattan distance; rational quadratic uses α = 1; cosine compares direction after scaling. The model data, point predictions, surfaces, boundaries and readouts are generated from experiment.py.
